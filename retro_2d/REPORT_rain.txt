KEY POINTS
----------
- Band levels follow wet-season rain. Wetter year -> higher
  backscatter (S1 VH, VV, Pu, alpha; PALSAR HV) and darker
  S2 bands. rho +0.9 / -0.9, S1 alpha rho 1.00.
- This fits the hypothesis. It also gives a probable cause
  for the year signature: 2019 (driest) is the extreme year.
- Class shares of the own-year models do NOT follow rain.
  No class has |rho| > 0.9; whole-histogram test: no relation.
- So the own-year models absorb the rain shift. Detrending
  their class shares by rain is not supported. Not done.
- Feature importances: no clear rain relation. PALSAR HV is
  rank 1 in 2019/21/23 and drops to 7-8 in 2024 (dry);
  2025 rank 3-4. Green gain falls with rain (rho -0.7). Weak.
- n = 5. Read rho sizes and consistency, not p values.

LIMITS
------
1. n = 5 years. Smallest two-sided exact p = 2/120 = 0.017.
   20 bands x 3 series: about 3 hits at p < 0.05 by chance.
2. Rain and other year effects (S1 1B loss Dec 2021, mosaic
   make-up) cannot be separated with 5 points.
3. Band medians come from the year-signature table
   (woody_thickening_splice_1), pindan fixed ground, not
   the mainland.
4. ERA5 is 0.25 deg (58 cells over the mainland).
5. Importances depend on each year's fit, not only the image.


NEXT (not done; needs a decision)
---------------------------------
The rain effect is in the bands, so a rain correction belongs
at the feature level, not on class shares. Possible test:
regress each band median on wet rain over the 5 years, remove
the fitted shift, and re-run the 2025 model on the corrected
earlier-year features (2025m, kappa now 0.29-0.43). If kappa
rises, rain explains part of the transfer failure.
